0.1 What is DeviceDNA?  
DeviceDNA is an AI -powered cybersecurity platform built specifically for the Internet of Things (IoT). 
Imagine a massive network with thousands of smart devices — smart cameras, thermostats, medical 
sensors, factory controllers. Because these devices often  lack traditional security software and run 
constantly, hackers can easily compromise them and remain undetected for months.  
 
DeviceDNA constantly monitors every device's network traffic, builds a Digital Twin of each device, and 
assigns a real -time Trust Score (0 –100). The moment a device acts strangely, its trust score drops, an 
alert fires, and the system can automatically isolate it.  
 
The core AI innovation is a two -tier Gaussian Mixture Variational Autoencoder (GMVAE) — a Global 
model that understands the entire network, and Specialized models per device class that understand fine -
grained behavior. This hierarchical architecture makes stealth attacks extremely difficult to hide.

1. Executive Summary  
DeviceDNA is a software -defined cybersecurity intelligence platform purpose -built for IoT network 
security. Traditional perimeter -based security (firewalls, signature detection) has become obsolete — IoT 
devices are compromised in ways that go undetected f or weeks. DeviceDNA applies a continuous, AI -
powered behavioral intelligence layer across every device, learning what each device should be doing 
and comparing reality against that model at every moment.  
 
The core architectural innovation is a Hierarchical  GMVAE (Gaussian Mixture Variational Autoencoder) 
system that replaces the simple VAE from earlier designs. A Global GMVAE learns network -wide 
behavior and routes telemetry to Specialized GMVAEs trained per device class. The comparison between 
global and l ocal understanding creates a powerful multi -signal anomaly detection engine that is extremely 
difficult for adversaries to fool simultaneously.  
 
Four Core Capabilities  
• Dynamic Trust Scoring: Real -time 0 –100 score per device, updated every 5 minutes based o n 
behavioral conformance, policy compliance, drift magnitude, peer comparison, and threat intel.  
• Policy Conformance & Drift Intelligence: Detects both hard violations (immediate, binary rule 
breaks) and soft drift (gradual, stealthy behavioral change) usin g CUSUM and LSTM.  
• Evidence -First Explainability: Every alert is accompanied by a human -readable Threat 
Intelligence Brief explaining what changed, why it matters, confidence level, and recommended 
action.  
• Autonomous Response Engine: Beyond detection — can isolate, sandbox, or throttle a device 
autonomously, functioning as a tier -1 SOC analyst that never sleeps.  
 
2. Problem Statement  
2.1 The IoT Security Crisis  
Global IoT device count surpassed 15 billion in  2024, projected to reach 29 billion by 2030. These 
devices — cameras, medical sensors, industrial controllers — operate continuously, transmit sensitive 
data, and run on minimal hardware that cannot support traditional security agents. The security challe nge 
is threefold:  
• Heterogeneity — Thousands of device types, OS variants, protocols, and vendors make unified 
policy enforcement nearly impossible.  
• Stealth — Modern IoT attackers (including nation -state APT groups) compromise devices slowly, 
staying beneat h detection thresholds for months.  
• Volume — A mid -sized enterprise might have 10,000+ IoT devices. No human team can monitor 
behavioral telemetry at that scale.

2.1 What Is GMVAE?  
DeviceDNA   |  AI-Powered IoT Cybersecurity Intellige nce Platform  |  Eclipse Hackathon 2025  
Comprehensive Technical Documentation — Updated May 2026  Page 11 A Gaussian Mixture Variational Autoencoder (GMVAE) extends the standard VAE by assuming the latent 
space has K Gaussian components (clusters) rather than a single Gaussian. This makes it naturally 
suited to modeling multiple device behavioral modes simulta neously.  
 
Standard VAE latent space: z ~ N(mu, sigma^2)  — single Gaussian, one mode  
GMVAE latent space: z ~ sum_k pi_k * N(mu_k, sigma_k^2)  — K Gaussians, K modes  
 
Where pi_k is the mixing coefficient (probability) for cluster k, mu_k is the cluster mean , sigma_k^2 is the 
cluster variance, and K is the number of device classes (K=6 for our 6 device types).  
 
2.2 STEP 1 — Global GMVAE: Whole -Network Understanding  
The Global GMVAE is trained on telemetry from ALL 50 devices across all 6 device classes. It le arns the 
global latent structure of the entire network.

Encoder

Global GMVAE Loss Function  
Loss_G = MSE(x, x_hat_G) + KL(q(z|x) || p(z)) + H_cluster  
Where MSE is the reconstruction error (anomaly signal at runtime), KL divergence regularizes the latent 
space to the mixture prior, and H_cluster is a cluster entropy regularizer that encourages confident cluster 
assignments.

• [pi_1, pi_2, ..., p i_K] — Cluster probabilities (routing confidence per device class)

2.3 STEP 2 — Cluster Routing  
The Global GMVAE's cluster probabilities act as a behavioral router. Telemetry is forwarded to the 
Specialized GMVAE corresponding to the highest -probability cluster.  
 
Routing Logic  
Example: Global GMVAE outputs [Camera: 92%, Sensor: 5%, Gateway: 3%] → Rou te to Camera Specialist 
GMVAE (confidence: 92%)  Example (ambiguous): [Camera: 40%, Sensor: 35%, Gateway: 25%] → Route to 
Camera GMVAE (marginally) → ALSO flag: low routing confidence = anomaly signal  
 
Routing confidence threshold: If max(pi_k) < 0.70, th e device's behavior is considered ambiguous — a 
potential stealth attack or identity drift. This ambiguity itself becomes an anomaly signal fed into the 
Fusion Engine.

The third scenario (globally acceptable but locally abnormal) is the most dangerous attack vector

Signal 2 — Latent Distance (Identity Drift)  
D_z = ||z - z_normal||  where z_ normal is the historical centroid of the device's latent vectors during 
baseline period.  
Large D_z means the device's latent representation has drifted from its enrolled behavioral identity. This 
catches gradual firmware compromise, hardware replacement, a nd slow behavior manipulation.  
 
D_z Value  Interpretation  Action  
< 1.0 Normal behavioral variation  No action  
1.0 – 2.5 Notable drift — monitor closely  Increase monitoring frequency  
> 2.5 Significant identity drift  Alert + CUSUM cross -check  
> 5.0 Possible firmware compromise  CRITICAL alert + isolate candidate  
 
Signal 3 — Cluster Confidence Anomaly  
If the Global GMVAE produces low max(pi_k), the device's behavior doesn't fit any known cluster well. 
This is a stealth attack pattern — adversaries often try to keep behavior 'between categories' to avoid 
triggering any single detector.  
Confidence Anomaly  Score = 1 - max(pi_k)  (0 = perfectly confident, 1 = totally uncertain)  
 
Signal 4 — Latent Entropy  
H = -sum(pi_k * log(pi_k))  across all cluster probabilities  
Maximum entropy (all clusters equally likely) = log(K) = log(6) ≈ 1.79. Minimum entropy (one cl uster = 
100%) = 0. High entropy means the device is behaving in a way that resembles multiple device classes 
simultaneously — unnatural and suspicious.

3. Isolation Forest — Statistical Outlier Detection  
DeviceDNA   |  AI-Powered IoT Cybersecurity Intellige nce Platform  |  Eclipse Hackathon 2025  
Comprehensive Technical Documentation — Updated May 2026  Page 15 3.1 Core Intuition — Why 'Isolation' Works  
Isolation Forest builds many random binary decision trees. For each tree, it picks a random feature and a 
random split value, dividing data into two groups. It repeats until each data point is isolated (alone in a 
leaf node).  
Key insight: anomalies are rar e and different, so they're isolated in very few splits. Normal points are 
similar to many others — they need many splits to separate. Short path = easy to isolate = anomaly.  
 
Anomaly Score = 2^( -average_path_length / c(n))  
where c(n) is the expected path length for a dataset of size n. Score near 1.0 = anomaly. Score near 0.5 
= normal.  
 
3.2 Implementation in DeviceDNA  
Unlike GMVAE (global + 6 specialist), Isolation Forests are trained per device class (6 models: camera, 
sensor, thermostat, access_control, medical, industrial). This provides a larger training set and allows 
new devices to be scored immediately without a 7 -day baseline. The IF excels at hard, structural 
anomalies — sudden dramatic changes like a camera suddenly using SSH, or a sensor contacti ng 15 
unique external IPs (normally 2).  
 
4. CUSUM Drift Engine — Detecting Slow, Stealthy Attacks  
4.1 The Full Math  
Step 1 — Z-score normalization: z_t = (x_t - mu) / sigma  
Step 2 — Accumulate deviations (two -sided CUSUM):  
  S+_t = max(0, S+_(t -1) + z_t - K)  [detects UPWARD drift]  
  S-_t = max(0, S -_(t-1) - z_t - K)  [detects DOWNWARD drift]  
Step 3 — Check threshold: If max(S+, S -) > H → DRIFT ALARM  
Step 4 — Normalize for trust engine: drift_score = min(1.0, max(S+, S -) / H) 
 
Parameter  Value  Meaning  
K (slack)  0.5 Ignores deviations within +/ - 0.5 sigma. Prevents false alarms from normal 
noise.  
H (threshold)  3.0 Cumulative z -score must exceed 3.0. Requires ~6 consecutive above -average 
readings.  
Clip limit  4.5 (H x 
1.5) Prevents cumulative sums from exploding if drift continues indefinitely.  
Tracked 
features  3 total_bytes, avg_packet_size, external_traffic_ratio — most exfil -sensitive dims.  
 
5. LSTM — Temporal Sequence Anomaly Detection  
LSTM (Long Short -Term Memory) is a recurrent neural network designed for sequential, time -dependent 
patterns. While GMVAE and Isolation Forest analyze a single 5 -minute snapshot, LSTM looks at the last

Architecture  
Input: Last 12 feature vectors (shape: batch x 12 x 14)  
LSTM(input=14, hidden=64, layers=2, dropout=0.2)

The 4 LS TM Gates  
Gate Formula  What It Controls  
Forget gate  f_t = sigma(Wf * [h_(t -1), x_t] + bf)  What fraction of old memory to keep (0=forget all, 
1=keep all)  
Input gate  i_t = sigma(Wi * [h_(t -1), x_t] + bi)  How much new information to store  
Cell update  C'_t = tanh(Wc * [h_(t -1), x_t] + bc)  Candidate new memory content  
Output gate  o_t = sigma(Wo * [h_(t -1), x_t] + bo)  What portion of memory to output now  
Cell state  C_t = f_t x C_(t -1) + i_t x C'_t  Updated long -term memory  
Hidden state  h_t = o_t x tanh(C_t)  Output at this timestep (fed to next timestep)

6. Graph Neural Network (GNN) — Network Topology Intelligence  
6.1 Graph Formulation  
The IoT network is modeled as a directed graph G = (V, E):  
• V (nodes) = IoT devices (50 nodes). Each node has a 14D feature vector: x_v in R^14  
• E (edges) = Communication flo ws between devices, directed, weighted by byte volume  
• Adjacency matrix A in R^(NxN) captures which devices talk to which  
Goal: For each node, predict Normal (0) or Anomalous (1) based on its features AND its position in the 
graph.  
 
6.2 GraphSAGE — Message Passing Mathematics  
Step 1: h_N(v)^(k) = MEAN({ h_u^(k -1) | u in N(v) })  — Aggregate neighbor features  
Step 2: h_v^(k) = ReLU( W^(k) · CONCAT( h_v^(k -1), h_N(v)^(k) ) )  — Transform  
Step 3: h_v^(k) = h_v^(k) / ||h_v^(k)||_2  — L2 normalize to prevent exploding embeddings  
 
6.3 DeviceDNA Architecture  
Layer 1: SAGEConv(14 → 32, normalize=True) → ReLU + Dropout(0.2)  
Layer 2: SAGEConv(32 → 32, normalize=True) → ReLU

8. Device DNA Fingerprinting  
DNA = [normalized_features(14D) || VAE_latent_mu(16D)] = 30D vector  
The DNA is a 30D vector combining raw behavioral features with the GMVAE's learned latent 
representation. It's the device's unique identity signature, updated every 5 minutes and compared against 
its enrolled baseline.  
 
Two Uses of DNA  
• Identity Verification: Compare live DNA to enrolled baseline using cosine similarity: 
cosine_sim(A,B) = (A·B) / (||A|| x ||B||)  
◦   Similarity > 0.85 → device identity confirmed, behavior consistent  
◦   Similarity 0.65 –0.85 → degraded behavior, possible configuration change  
◦   Similarity < 0.65 → possible firmware compromise or hardware replacement → ALERT  
• Unknown Device Classification: When a new device joins, compare its DNA to class -average 
DNA of all  6 known device types. If max similarity < 0.65 → classified as 'unknown_alien_device' 
→ immediate alert.

protocol ratios converge to the defined probabilities by the Law of Large Numbers. Batch generation:

often than Thermostats (15 flows/5min) in each batch.
